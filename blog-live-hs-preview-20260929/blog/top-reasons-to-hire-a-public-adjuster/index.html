Top Reasons to Hire a Public Adjuster

Written by joe suskind April 1, 2020 10 AM

Hiring a public adjuster is a decision, not a default. A PA works for the policyholder: documenting damage and negotiating the claim. Company and independent adjusters typically work the carrier side. Carriers are for-profit; that alone is not a reason to hire anyone — mismatch between your loss and the claim file is.

This page is a decision framework: when hiring usually helps, when it may be optional, and what to expect on fees. For five concrete day-to-day benefits of PA work, see the sibling post Top 5 Benefits for Hiring a Public Insurance Adjuster.

When hiring a public adjuster usually helps

1. The loss is large or complex

Multiple rooms, structural issues, commercial property, contents inventories, mold secondary damage, or several trades on one estimate — these claims reward systematic documentation. A licensed PA is built for that workload.

2. The first offer feels low relative to real estimates

You have contractor bids or unit costs that do not match the carrier’s sheet, and the gap is material. A PA can re-scope, challenge omissions, and negotiate line by line. That is advocacy — not a guarantee the carrier will meet every line.

3. The claim was underpaid or only partially paid

Lowball offers, thin first scopes, and partial payments often need a reopen/supplement package: reason-code analysis, policy comparison, and new evidence of missed or underpriced damage. See Why Reopen an Insurance Claim? and How Do I Reopen an Insurance Claim?.

4. You cannot devote the hours the claim needs

Deadlines, proof of loss, inspections, and correspondence pile up while you are displaced or running a business. Paying a contingency fee for claim management can be rational when the opportunity cost of DIY is high.

5. You want claim-language advocacy without starting in litigation

Many disputes resolve in the claim process. A PA is not a substitute for an attorney when you need a lawsuit, bad-faith case strategy, or courtroom work — but for scope and amount disputes still inside the claim, a PA is often the right first specialist. Escalate to counsel when legal rights, EUO traps, or suit deadlines dominate.

When hiring may be optional

Hiring is often optional when:

  • The loss is small, clearly covered, and the carrier’s offer already matches a solid local contractor estimate you trust
  • You are comfortable reading the policy and managing a short documentation loop yourself
  • The only remaining dispute is a pure legal coverage question your attorney is already handling

Even then, if you bring anyone in, verify they are a licensed public adjuster in the property’s state — see How Can I Find a Public Adjuster Near Me?.

Contingency fees — alignment, not magic

Most public adjusters charge a percentage of the claim recovery under a written contract (rules and caps can vary by state). That usually means:

  • Limited or no upfront hourly bill for claim handling
  • Incentive to see the claim through
  • You should still read exclusions, expense items, and what happens if the claim recovers little

Ask for the fee agreement before you sign. Contingency is a financing structure — not proof of outcome size.
