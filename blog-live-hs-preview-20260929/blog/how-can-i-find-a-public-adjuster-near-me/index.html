How Can I Find a Public Adjuster Near Me?

Written by joe suskind September 28, 2019 6 PM

Searching “public adjuster near me” usually means you have property damage and an insurance claim that is already stressful. A public adjuster works for you, the policyholder — not for the insurance company. This guide shows how to find and vet one, so you can decide with a clear checklist instead of a sales pitch.

Staff, independent, and public adjusters — who works for whom?

TypeWho pays themWhose interests they represent
Company (staff) adjusterThe insurance carrierThe carrier’s claim evaluation
Independent adjusterOften contracted by the carrierUsually still assigned by the carrier side
Public adjusterTypically paid by the policyholder (often contingency)You — documenting damage and negotiating the claim

If you want a deeper breakdown, see UCS’s guide on the differences between types of adjusters.

Step 1 — Confirm they are licensed where the property is

Public adjusting is regulated by state. Before you hire anyone from a “near me” search:

  1. Ask for their full legal name and license number.
  2. Verify the license on your state insurance department (or DFS / DOI) public lookup — not only on their website.
  3. Confirm the license covers public adjusting (not only an agent or broker license).
  4. If the property is in one state and the firm markets nationally, ask which states they are authorized to handle.

UCS works claims in multiple operating states (including New York, New Jersey, Connecticut, Pennsylvania, and Florida). Rules differ by state — verify locally even if a firm is national.

Step 2 — Red flags to walk away from

  • Pressure to sign immediately or “today only” contingency deals.
  • Guarantees of a maximum, “highest,” or guaranteed settlement size.
  • Unwillingness to show a license number or written fee agreement.
  • Advice to misrepresent damage or inflate inventories.
  • No clear explanation of fees, expenses, or what happens if the claim settles low.
  • Only a chat widget / out-of-state call center with no local claim process.

Honest firms explain the process, put fees in writing, and do not over-promise.

Step 3 — Questions to ask before you hire

  1. Are you licensed as a public adjuster in the state where my property is?
  2. Who will actually inspect and document my loss — you or a junior adjuster or associate on your staff?
  3. How do you charge (contingency % of what, on what base)? What expenses might I pay separately?
  4. What is your plan for documentation (photos, estimates, inventories, expert reports)?
  5. How do you handle carrier deadlines, inspections, and examinations under oath?
  6. Can you walk me through a recent claim type like mine (water, fire, wind, roof, etc.) without naming private clients?
  7. When would you recommend I speak with an attorney instead of (or in addition to) a PA?

Write the answers down. Compare at least two licensed options when time allows.

Step 4 — When hiring a public adjuster usually makes sense

Hiring often helps when:

  • The loss is large or complex (multiple rooms, structural, mold secondary damage, commercial).
  • The first offer feels low relative to visible damage and estimates.
  • The claim was underpaid, closed early, or only partially paid and you still disagree.
  • You do not have time to manage estimates, supplements, and carrier correspondence alone.
  • You want someone who speaks claims language (scopes, depreciation, coverage framing) on your side of the table.

Hiring may be optional when the loss is small, clearly covered, and the carrier’s offer already matches a solid contractor estimate you trust — still verify the license if you bring anyone in.

Step 5 — What a serious PA should do early

A competent public adjuster typically:

  1. Reviews the policy and loss notice.
  2. Inspects and documents the damage thoroughly.
  3. Helps you prepare for the carrier’s inspection (often: do not complete permanent repairs that hide evidence before inspection — temporary mitigation to prevent further damage is different).
  4. Builds a supportable claim package and negotiates with the carrier.
  5. Keeps you informed without burying you in jargon.

You still own major decisions (settlement acceptance, attorney involvement, litigation). A PA is an advocate and claim specialist — not a magician and not a court.

Multi-state note

“Near me” is about where the damaged property sits and where the adjuster is licensed — not only who ranks first in a map pack. A firm can serve several states if properly licensed; a local pin on Google is not a license check.
